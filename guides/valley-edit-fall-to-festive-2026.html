The Valley Edit

From Fall to Festive

November Happenings & Local Favorites in the Shenandoah Valley

Presented by Cedar Escape

Home / Things to Do / The Valley Edit

There's something special about November in the Shenandoah Valley. The mountains are settling into their late-autumn colors, local markets are filling with handmade treasures, and the first holiday lights are beginning to appear.

From a morning spent discovering local artisans to Thanksgiving traditions and festive evenings at Massanutten, November offers plenty of reasons to make a little more time for the people and places you love.

Here's what we're looking forward to this month.

In Season

A Slower Kind of Saturday

Before the holiday rush begins, make time for a little local tradition.

The Harrisonburg Farmers Market is open Tuesdays and Saturdays throughout November, offering an easy way to explore the area's growers, makers, and small businesses.

Wander through Turner Pavilion, browse what's in season, and pick up something local to enjoy during your stay.

The market's November schedule includes Tuesday and Saturday markets from 8 AM–1 PM.

Whether you're planning breakfast, shopping for a holiday gathering, or simply enjoying a morning downtown, it's a lovely way to experience Harrisonburg beyond the usual attractions.

Featured Experience

Handmade for the Holidays

Festive Fall Open-Air Market at Green Valley Book Fair

  • Saturday, November 14
  • 9 AM–2 PM
  • Mount Crawford

There's something about finding a gift at a local market that makes it feel a little more meaningful.

This November, Green Valley Book Fair brings together more than 50 local businesses, artists, crafters, and bakers for its Festive Fall Open-Air Market.

Expect everything from handmade jewelry and home décor to artwork, accessories, and sweet treats.

And because the market takes place outside Green Valley Book Fair, it's the perfect opportunity to browse the book fair's collection of discounted books, puzzles, and gifts while you're there.

Why we're featuring it: It combines holiday shopping, local creativity, and a little something for nearly everyone—without requiring a packed itinerary.

Make a morning of it, enjoy lunch, and leave room for an afternoon of exploring the valley.

Eat, Sip & Something Sweet

A Little Taste of November

Good food deserves a place in the itinerary, especially when a local market makes it easy to sample something new.

At the November 14 Festive Fall Open-Air Market, the announced food lineup includes:

  • Catrea's BBQ & More
  • Shenandoah Valley Reserve
  • The Sweet Spot Dessert Truck

That means you can work a savory bite, something to sip, and a sweet finish into your market visit without needing to make several separate stops.

For another low-key food outing, stop by the Harrisonburg Farmers Market and see what its seasonal vendors have available.

Our tipCome hungry, browse first, and make time for dessert. Vacation calories have a remarkable talent for finding their own itinerary.

Worth the Detour

Get Creative at Friendly City Clay

Holiday shopping is fun. Making something yourself? That's a different kind of memory.

At Friendly City Clay & Art Center in Harrisonburg, creative workshops offer visitors a chance to slow down, try something new, and leave with a piece of the experience.

On November 21 from 1–3 PM, the studio has scheduled Linocut Card Making: Holiday Greetings, a beginner-friendly workshop where participants ages 16 and older can create hand-printed holiday cards.

It's a particularly nice option for couples, friends, or anyone looking for an afternoon activity beyond the slopes.

Friendly City Clay is also one of Cedar Escape's local experience partners, with a guest benefit available on eligible scheduled workshops.
