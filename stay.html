Where Everyone Sleeps

Room by room, floor by floor.

The most common question before booking a group trip: where does everyone actually sleep? Here's exactly what each bedroom looks like, and which bathroom serves it.

Main Floor
Emerald Room
King-Size Bed · Sleeps 2

Cedar Escape's primary suite — a rich emerald accent wall, a channel-tufted headboard, a built-in dual-monitor desk, and its own private bathroom just steps away. Located on the main level, so no stairs between this room and the kitchen or living area.

Emerald Room — Private En-Suite
Walk-In Shower · Double Vanity

A double vanity, a glass walk-in shower, and a wall-mounted towel warmer — Cedar Escape's only en-suite bathroom, private to the Emerald Room.

Pine Room
Queen Bed · Sleeps 2

Warm wood paneling and a cozy queen bed, with its own built-in desk nook — handy for anyone who needs to get a little work done before rejoining the group.

Adventure Room
2 Twin Beds · Sleeps 2

A bold blue accent wall and two twin beds — built for kids, teens, or anyone happy to grab a twin instead of sharing.

Main-Floor Hall Bathroom
Walk-In Shower · Single Vanity

Serves both the Pine and Adventure Rooms — this isn't an en-suite to either one. A light blue bath with a walk-in shower and single vanity, just down the hall from both.

Basement / Lower Level
Mountain Room
King-Size Bed · Sleeps 2

A king bed set against a hand-painted mountain silhouette — the most requested room in the house.

Double Bunk Room
2 Twin-Over-Twin Bunks · Sleeps 4

Two full sets of twin-over-twin bunks under a hand-painted mountain mural — the favorite room for the kids (or the friend group not ready to admit they still love bunk beds).

Lower-Level Bathroom
Tub / Shower Combo · Single Vanity

Cedar Escape's only bathtub — serves both lower-level bedrooms, right around the corner from the game room.

9 real beds plus a downstairs sleeper sofa, sleeping up to 14 comfortably.
